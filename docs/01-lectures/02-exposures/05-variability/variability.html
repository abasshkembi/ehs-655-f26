Basic concepts

  • If exposures varied over time, but not among people, we could…

    • Monitor single individual continuously

    • Make multiple measurements randomly distributed over time on that individual

  • If exposures varied between people, but not over time, we could…

    • Make single instantaneous measure on every person

    • More people better; repeated measurements not

Temporal variability

  • Basic dimension of variability at individual level

    • Change jobs or residence

    • Quit smoking

    • Retire

  • Exposures vary within and between days, months, years, decades

  • Congenital attributes may be only exposure truly fixed at individual level

    • However, many epidemiological methods require exposures to be treated as fixed (e.g., case-control)

Measures to summarize exposure variability

    • Standard deviation (\(\sigma\))

  • Categorical data

    • Frequency table

Another measure to describe variability

Standard error of the mean

\[ SE(\bar{x})=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \]

How does this differ from the standard deviation?

  • SD is measure of dispersion of data around mean

  • SD stays the same (on average) when sample size increases

  • SE describes accuracy of sample mean as estimate of population mean

  • SE decreases with increasing sample size

Methods for dealing with exposure variability

  • Ignore variability

  • Use maximum or minimum value

  • Use average value

  • Disaggregate variability

    • Develop distribution(s)
